# Demonstration and pilot worksheet

## Three-minute demonstration

0:00–0:25 — Introduce the target learner and problem. State that this is a four-lesson prototype.
0:25–0:55 — Select Physics. Explain the equal-height and no-air-resistance assumptions in the range equation.
0:55–1:30 — Open projectile motion, compare 30° and 60° at the same speed, and return to the tutor. State that settings are not automatically shared.
1:30–2:00 — Try the question, request a hint, submit an answer and inspect feedback.
2:00–2:30 — Show Chemistry, Mathematics and the Biology guided investigation. Download the browser's practice record.
2:30–3:00 — If configured and tested, ask one live AI question. Otherwise explicitly identify the guided demonstration and say live AI configuration remains outstanding. Close with actual pilot findings or the proposed test plan, depending on what has been completed.

## Five-day capstone plan

Day 1: integrate and verify the full main task on phone and desktop.
Day 2: observe five target learners attempting the task without help.
Day 3: group difficulties and review all content with subject specialists.
Day 4: fix the most frequent difficulty and retest with two or three learners.
Day 5: complete the factual report, demonstration and portfolio description.

## Submission checklist

- Working link or locally demonstrated prototype, as required by the organiser.

- Three activity routes tested in the running application.
- Five anonymous usability observations and actual completion counts.
- Revised prototype and retest notes.
- Teacher review of scientific statements and generated answers.
- Short demonstration and factual project summary.
